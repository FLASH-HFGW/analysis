# Acquisition hardware: specifications, evidence and open questions

Updated 17 November 2026. This inventory documents the supplied original acquisition diagram and manufacturer documents. It does not change the step 6 reference, its absolute normalization, the scientific reconstruction, or the phenomenological step 8 response. No new fits, transfer functions or simulations were introduced. Laboratory characterization is future work; it cannot be performed at this stage.

## Evidence convention

- **SPEC**: manufacturer specification or typical performance under the manufacturer's stated conditions, not an in-situ measurement.
- **SETTING**: experimental configuration from acquisition records, or an explicitly identified operator communication. Generator setpoints are not delivered powers at mixer ports.
- **MEASURED**: existing experimental/diagnostic results, retained without refitting.
- **HYPOTHESIS**: interpretation requiring a discriminating measurement.
- **UNKNOWN**: not established by the supplied documents. Missing specifications are not assumed to be zero.

Page numbers below are PDF page numbers; the original PowerPoint uses slide numbers. Sources are original documents, not reseller summaries. Mini-Circuits originals were retrieved from the manufacturer on 17 November 2026; URLs and SHA-256 checksums are in [manufacturer_downloads.json](manufacturer_downloads.json). The supplied diagram and datasheets are retained alongside this inventory.

## Original chain and routing limitations

Source: [QUAX_multimode_v2.pptx](QUAX_multimode_v2.pptx), slides 1–2. The simplified presentation diagram is a functional reconstruction of this drawing, not an independently verified wiring diagram.

Cryogenic cavity → circulator/TWPA/HEMT chain → room-temperature RF amplification → primary Marki IQ mixer → FEMTO amplifier on each I/Q branch → one four-way ZSC splitter per quadrature → three mode paths. TM010 goes directly to the final low-pass/ADC; TM011 and TM012 pass through their band-pass and second mixer, then the final low-pass/ADC. There are six final I/Q channels. The fourth output of each four-way splitter is not documented.

The original I-side SIF-40+/SBP-101+ labels appear transposed relative to their passbands and intended mode frequencies; the Q-side assignment is consistent. Functional routing is SIF-40+ for TM011 and SBP-101+ for TM012. Installed routing needs confirmation from records or future inspection. Original channel-number labels also differ from the logical interleaved I/Q pairs used by the analysis; no analysis mapping is changed here. The RF amplifier block lists two models separated by a slash: this is not evidence that both are installed in series.

## Experimental evidence retained without new processing

**SETTING:** the existing run 680 chain has primary IF centers approximately 1.250, 44.971 and 105.313 MHz and final IF centers 1.250, 1.248 and 1.256 MHz. These are different coordinates. The candidate splitter is before the secondary mixers; a suppression around zero of primary IF therefore need not appear at zero of every final mode coordinate.

**MEASURED:** the existing DC diagnostic identifies an approximately 25 kHz-wide TM010 spectral depression and a negative autocorrelation component; a stable complex offset alone does not establish the origin of the broad depression. Existing results and their original subtraction/PSD conventions remain unchanged. Source: step 7 DC diagnostic (commit 29c10ac) and step 8 existing products under `analysis-work/Run2/simulation/`; these are external numerical products, not manufacturer specifications.

**HYPOTHESIS:** ZSC-4-1+ splitters are plausible candidates because performance below 100 kHz is unspecified. This is a motivation for characterization, not an identified pole, measured attenuation or attribution of fc=12.5 kHz and epsilon=0.02. The step 8 response remains a phenomenological description. Its agreement in some observables and discrepancies in others are scientific results. Finite LF attenuation and an independent added-noise floor can share the same second-order spectrum; spectral agreement alone cannot locate the source.

**SPEC versus HYPOTHESIS:** FEMTO 10 Hz AC coupling is far below the observed width. Gain-independent manufacturer response weakens an intrinsic gain-dependent LF cutoff interpretation. It does not prove that the entire loaded experimental chain was identical between calibration and acquisition, nor establish the actual coupling/bandwidth switch states. Its specified 20 kHz noise corner is an additive-noise property, not evidence for a 20 kHz suppression.

## Frozen normalization and separation of calibration from acquisition

The measured kz curves remain the mapping between the physical toy and the real chain. No per-component datasheet loss/gain is added on top of kz, and no gain is tuned for agreement. Step 6 parameters, ADC convention, measured calibration curves and existing absolute-normalization ledger are unchanged. ADC-domain volts and nominal input-referred quantities retain their separate labels. The 60/20 dB FEMTO difference is an experimental setting difference, not proof of a changed LF transfer function. Calibration and science chain responses must be measured separately before deciding what a correction would mean. A hypothetical science suppression must not be counted twice if already included in kz. No such correction is implemented here.

## Proposed hardware characterization — future work only

No additional laboratory measurements can currently be performed. The following discriminating measurements are proposals, not prerequisites for reporting the present discrepancies:

1. Recover installed model/serials, wiring, port terminations, ADC ranges, FEMTO AC/DC and 10/100 MHz switch states, amplifier gains/bias and LO settings separately for calibration and acquisition. Resolve the original I-side BPF labels and ADC channel-label mismatch using records first.
2. Measure complex response of each ZSC-4-1+ from well below the depression through and above 100 kHz, with actual source/load impedances and fourth-port termination. Compare I and Q, outputs, direct bypass and complete installed primary-IF paths. This tests whether the splitter can account for width, depth and phase response without assigning a manufacturer band edge as a pole.
3. Measure FEMTO main-output response at 20 and 60 dB, both relevant coupling/bandwidth settings and actual loads; record additive output-noise PSD, offsets, compression and I/Q balance. Keep the 100 kHz monitor output distinct from the main output.
4. Inject the same calibrated small signal/noise at defined locations before/after FEMTO and splitters; compare full-path and bypass responses at the three primary IFs and final ADC paths. Record delivered mixer LO powers. Use terminated-input/noise measurements to distinguish upstream filtered noise from downstream added noise.
5. Measure I/Q amplitude/phase balance, quadrature correlations and DC stability with controlled terminations; acquire sufficiently long records for mean-variance scaling and sufficiently fine frequency spacing for the central depression. Compare calibration and acquisition settings with identical conversion conventions.

None of these proposals introduces a new fitted response, new simulation or retroactive improvement of the step 6 reference.
